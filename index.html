Diligence list · operations

  • Lease structure — NNN vs gross defines who carries the opex
  • Tenant concentration & lease expiry schedule
  • TI / LC, free rent and downtime during lease-up
  • Capex reserves — roof, yards, fire systems

Diligence list · capital

  • Acquisition costs — ~3% transfer tax in Peru, buyer-paid
  • Local transaction comps vs $850/m² and replacement cost
  • Financing structure and exit tax treatment
  • Exit cap evidence — Lima transaction yields, if available

06 — Facts, assumptions and scope

A model is only as honest as its labels.

Market data · sourced

  • JLL, Lima Logistics Report H1 2026: absorption >60,000 m² (+58% YoY, −37% HoH); vacancy −1.1 pp; A–B spread 45%
  • Cushman & Wakefield, Peru MarketBeat S2 2025: 1.98M m² inventory; 11.5% vacancy; ~$6.40/m²/mo asking rent

Model assumptions · labeled

  • $5.90/m²/mo starting rent · 90→95% occupancy · 3% rent growth
  • 18% opex · 7.0% exit cap · 2% selling costs · $8.5M illustrative price
  • Scope: unlevered, before taxes, acquisition costs, capex, TI/LC
07

07 — The takeaway

“The point wasn’t to prove the deal works. The goal was to find out what would have to be true for it to work.”

$8.5M acquisition → 7.14% modeled unlevered IRR
10% target IRR → ~$7.57M maximum acquisition price

Now you have moved the levers yourself. That is the whole exercise: not the headline number, but knowing which questions must be answered before anyone signs anything.
